description: A timeline of what to prepare before you leave, from one year ahead to your last day and after.

Leaving well takes about a year. The point is not the paperwork on your last day, but
making sure the work you did stays usable by the people who stay: data that can be
found, code that can be run, and results that can be reproduced without you.

Tick the boxes as you go — they are remembered in your browser.

:::note
Dates and names below follow the usual case of a PhD student finishing a thesis. If you
are on a shorter contract, compress the timeline but keep the same order. TODO: confirm
the administrative steps with the secretariat.
:::

## 12 months before — Plan the end of your thesis

- [ ] **Agree on a defence date** with your promoter, and work back from it to set a writing schedule.
- [ ] **Check when your contract and funding end**, and whether the two match your defence date.
- [ ] **Check the administrative requirements** of your doctoral school (credits, private defence, deposit deadlines). TODO: link to the ULB doctoral school page.
- [ ] **If you are not a Belgian or EU citizen**, check how your residence permit is tied to your contract.

## 6 months before — Organise the handover

This is the part people underestimate. Someone will have to pick up your data, and that
person is rarely there on your last day.

- [ ] **Identify who takes over** each of your projects, with your promoter.
- [ ] **Write down where your data lives**: every path on `/mnt/iribhm`, every cloud bucket, every external disk.
- [ ] **Document what the data actually is**: samples, conditions, processing steps, known problems. A `README.md` next to the data beats a conversation.
- [ ] **Walk your successor through a full analysis**, from raw data to figure, while you are still around to answer questions.

## 3 months before — Clean up data and code

- [ ] **Delete intermediate files** you know are useless: failed runs, duplicated downloads, old test outputs.
- [ ] **Move anything worth keeping out of your home folder**, into the shared project folders. Home folders are personal and get cleaned up.
- [ ] **Check that your repositories run from a clean checkout**, with a `README.md` saying how..

## 1 month before — Administration

- [ ] **Update your entry on the "Meet the Team" page**, or ask for it to be removed.
- [ ] **Set up mail forwarding**, and tell collaborators which address to use afterwards.
- [ ] **Return borrowed equipment**: laptop, screens, hard drives, books.
- [ ] **Settle outstanding expenses** (travel reimbursements, conference fees).
- [ ] **Ask what happens to your cluster account** and for how long it stays open. TODO: current policy?

## The last week — Final handover

- [ ] **Run a final backup** of anything personal you want to keep, and check that you can actually open it.
- [ ] **Hand over ownership** of repositories, shared documents and cloud projects.
- [ ] **Sit down with your successor one last time**, with your documentation open, and let them drive.
- [ ] **Leave a text file in your project folders** with your personal email and a one-paragraph summary of where things stand.
- [ ] **Remove personal credentials** from shared machines: SSH keys, tokens, saved passwords.

## After you leave

- [ ] **Stay reachable for paper revisions** — reviewers come back months later.
- [ ] **Keep your ORCID and affiliations up to date** on papers still in the pipeline.
- [ ] **Answer the occasional "where is that file?" email.** It will happen, and it is much easier if you did the steps above.
