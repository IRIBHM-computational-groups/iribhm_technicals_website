## Lab meeting

**Every Thursday, 10:00–12:00.** Two things happen, in this order:

1. **A presentation of about an hour**, given by one person. The speaker changes every
   week, so the turn comes back to you a few months later.
2. **The grand tour**, where everyone says what they did during the week. The lab is
   split into **team A** and **team B**, and each team does its grand tour every other
   week — so your own turn comes around every two weeks.

The presentation is not meant to be polished: work in progress, a method that failed, or
a result you do not know how to interpret are all good material. The grand tour is short
by design, a few minutes per person.

## Journal club

**Occasionally**, a small group presents a paper they found interesting. It takes the
place of the usual slot rather than being an extra meeting.


## Lab retreat

**Once a year, in September.** Three days and two nights away from the lab, so it takes
up a couple of working days — keep the dates free once they are announced.


## l33t re treats

Despite the name, this one has nothing to do with the lab retreat above: it is a short
session, not a trip.

**Occasionally**, whenever someone has technical tools worth showing to the rest of the
group — for the geeks. There is no fixed schedule: it happens when somebody proposes a
topic. Past editions covered, for instance, [large language models in the
lab](../large_language_models/).

If you want to present something, say so and a date gets set.

## Knowing when it is your turn

The order of presentations is kept on Teams, in
`/Share/presentations/ordre_of_presentation.xlsx`. The same file says **which team you
are in**, A or B, and therefore which weeks you do the grand tour.

If you have just arrived and cannot find yourself in it, ask to be added — see the
[onboarding checklist](../newcomers/).
